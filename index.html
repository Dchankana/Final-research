Project Scope

Research Domain

This section captures the literature survey, research gap, research problem & solution, research objectives, and the methodology used throughout the project.

Literature Survey

Speech and pronunciation difficulties are common among children and teenagers and can significantly affect their confidence and communication skills. In Sri Lanka, many families cannot easily access speech therapy due to high costs and limited availability of specialists.

This issue mainly affects children with speech difficulties, their parents, and schools that lack proper speech support resources. Research into AI-powered speech analysis has seen rapid growth globally, yet low-resource language environments like Sinhala remain underserved.

Recent advances in deep learning — particularly Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), Siamese networks, and transformer-based architectures — have shown promising results in speech recognition and pronunciation scoring tasks. However, most existing solutions target English or other high-resource languages, leaving a critical gap for Sri Lankan children.

  • Existing tools lack support for Sinhala phonemes and local accents.
  • Gamified speech therapy apps have shown higher engagement in pediatric populations.
  • Audio quality enhancement preprocessing significantly improves downstream model accuracy.
  • Siamese networks for pronunciation comparison outperform rule-based scoring in child speech contexts.

🌍 Low-Resource Language Support

No comprehensive AI speech improvement system exists for Sinhala or Tamil-speaking children in Sri Lanka. Existing tools are predominantly English-centric.

🎙️ Audio Quality in Real-World Settings

Most research assumes clean audio input. There is limited work on noise-robust speech analysis for child speech captured via low-cost mobile devices in home or classroom settings.

📊 Pronunciation Scoring for Children

Pronunciation scoring models trained on adult speech do not generalize well to child voice characteristics such as higher pitch, varying tempo, and shorter phoneme durations.

👨‍👩‍👧 Parent & Therapist Integration

Existing apps rarely provide structured dashboards for parents and speech therapists to track progress, communicate, and receive personalized AI recommendations.

🎮 Gamification & Engagement

There is insufficient research on the long-term engagement effects of gamified speech therapy specifically for Sri Lankan children aged 5–17.

🤖 Personalized Adaptive Learning

No current system in the Sri Lankan context uses reinforcement-learning-based adaptive curricula that automatically adjust difficulty based on individual child progress.

Research Problem

How can an AI-based system accurately analyze and improve the speech and pronunciation of children and teenagers in Sri Lanka, overcoming barriers of cost, accessibility, and language resource scarcity?

Over 1 million children in Sri Lanka are estimated to have some form of speech difficulty. Access to qualified speech-language pathologists is extremely limited outside major cities, and private therapy is financially out of reach for most families. Schools lack structured speech support programs, and no affordable digital alternative exists for local languages.

Proposed Solution — TalkTutor

TalkTutor is a mobile and web-based AI speech improvement platform specifically designed for children and teenagers in Sri Lanka. It combines four AI-powered components:

  • Audio Quality Enhancement — Cleans and enhances recorded speech using signal processing and ML classifiers before analysis.
  • Pronunciation Accuracy Scoring — Uses a Siamese network trained on child–parent audio pairs to score pronunciation quality and provide feedback.
  • Word Classification & Speech Delay Analysis — Identifies individual word recognition issues and detects speech delay patterns using CNN-based classifiers.
  • Personalized Recommendations & Parent Dashboard — A Decision Tree policy model predicts optimal learning actions and delivers recommendations to parents and therapists in real time.

System Methodology

The proposed TalkTutor system follows an end-to-end machine learning pipeline organized into four major components, each developed independently and integrated via a unified mobile/web backend.

Component 01 – Audio Quality & Improvement Analysis (Toussaint I M R)
Raw speech audio is captured through the mobile app. The pipeline preprocesses audio by extracting acoustic quality features, then trains and evaluates multiple classifiers (Random Forest, SVM, XGBoost) using train/val/test splits with cross-validation. The best model (selected by F1 score) classifies audio quality, and enhancement is applied accordingly.

Component 02 – Pronunciation Accuracy & Scoring (Chankana D G L)
A Siamese neural network is trained on child–parent speech pairs with ground-truth human pronunciation scores. The model learns phonetic similarity through contrastive learning, supporting hard cross-word negatives and identity samples. Optional audio augmentations improve robustness. Output includes a confidence-calibrated score ("Excellent", "Good", "Needs Practice") per word.

Component 03 – Word Classification & Speech Delay Analysis (Ekanayaka M E G D P)
A dataset of Sinhala word audio clips is processed via feature extraction (MFCCs, spectrograms). An 80/20 stratified split generates train/test CSVs. Multiple classifiers are trained with repeated cross-validation; the best model is evaluated on the test set and saved with a full performance report. A Mystery Match game UI provides real-time word-matching practice.

Component 04 – Recommendations & Parent Communication (Nadeeshan I A D D)
Student practice sessions are simulated to create a labelled dataset for a Decision Tree policy model (policy_model.pkl). The model predicts the optimal next learning action per student. A parent/therapist dashboard visualises progress and enables chat-based communication. Account creation and role-based access control are implemented for parents, children, and therapists.
